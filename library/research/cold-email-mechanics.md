# Cold email mechanics: who to copy and how good the evidence is

**Decision:** copy the mechanics of established agency practice for a done-for-you AI offer: plain text, no open-tracking pixel, three to four follow-ups, business-hours sends, one new campaign angle per day, and a free deliverable built from the prospect's own situation as the reason to reply. Do not copy tool vendors (their marketing is the software, not an offer to a buyer).

| practice | what it is | evidence quality |
|---|---|---|
| plain text, no pixel | avoids spam filters that read tracking images | practitioner consensus; no controlled study found |
| 3-4 follow-ups | most replies come after touch 1 | vendor-published reply curves; UNSURE |
| free work from their own post | the message carries proof of effort | practitioner reports; strongest when the work is a demo of THEIR product |
| personalized video | a demo of the prospect's own product lifts replies (one 200-email test: 28.3% vs 4.6% generic) | vendor claim, small sample; UNSURE |
| link in touch 1 from a new domain | looks like phishing to filters and readers | deliverability vendors agree; so touch 1 has no link |

**Two disagreements, resolved.** Volume (thousands before judging vs ten hyper-personalized): follow list size; with about ten qualified targets a cycle, run the personalized version and measure the first ten. Link timing: touch 1 plain, the single link arrives in touch 2.

**Gaps.** No independent reply-rate data exists for any source above; measure our own first ten sends and publish the numbers.
**Sources:** Belkins public cold-email benchmarks and agency comparison; Prospeo and DemoWalk video-in-email write-ups; deliverability guidance from Smartlead, Instantly and Firstsales; plus the private course material indexed under library/sources (overlay only).
