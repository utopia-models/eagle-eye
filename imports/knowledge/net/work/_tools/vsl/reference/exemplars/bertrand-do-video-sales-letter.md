## Beat sheet (paraphrase of video-sales-letter/references/story-framework.md)
1. Hook: show the input-to-output transformation quickly.
2. Problem and promise: the buyer's situation and what the video will resolve.
3. Reason to keep watching: a specific demonstration, never "something exciting".
4. Friction: the time, cost or repeated work in the current approach.
5. Mechanism: why this system changes the process.
6. Demo: a real input, the intermediate state, the finished output.
7. Next step: connect the output to the buyer's job.
8. Proof: verifiable output or relevant maker history.
9. Offer and value: exact scope, framed against a supported alternative.
10. Guarantee: only terms that are real.
11. Boundary: what the system does not automate, when it answers an objection.
12. Action: one clear next step.

## What to copy
- The claims ledger: before scripting, list every claim (numbers, limits, speed, compatibility) with its source; a script line without a ledger row is cut. Our equivalent is the brief's `capabilities` list.
- Demo-first ordering when the visible transformation is the strongest evidence (our order: roadmap, architecture, live demo, next step).
- The audit: read every spoken line with no visuals, resolve vague referents ("this", "it", "another"), and check that the demo proves the exact claim.

## What not to copy
- Its advice to use contractions for a spoken feel: Tyler's rule for takes is no contractions.
- Offer, bonus, price and urgency beats: a technical pitch to one hiring manager carries none of them.
