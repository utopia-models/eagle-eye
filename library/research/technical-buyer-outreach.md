# Writing to technical buyers

Pages fetched and read 2026-11-27. Each quote is word for word (25 words or fewer) and belongs to its author; the rest is our own words.
Limits are stated at the end. Copy the move, never another sender's facts.

## 1. Name one real workflow, in words you can defend
> "Technical jargon turns engineers away. What performs better is speaking to a specific use case or workflow in language that feels authentic."

[Lavender, emailing technical buyers](https://lavender.ai/blog/benchmark-learnings-emailing-technical-buyers). Move: one workflow from their world, said plainly. A term you cannot explain stays out.

## 2. No hype words, one observation about their system
> "Don't use buzzwords you can't back up. Don't write more than 100 words."

> "Lead with a company-level technical observation — a migration, an architecture decision, a security posture shift, a product launch."

Same page. Move: open on something true about their architecture or their post; keep the proof concrete.

## 3. Do the work first, then show it
> "When we were done, we'd send them a cold email, including the revamped documentation."

[Mintlify, things that do not scale](https://www.mintlify.com/blog/things-that-do-not-scale). Move: build the first piece for them before contact.

> "We noticed these three flows in your product that seem hard for users to do."

[A founder describing his cold video outreach, SaaS Club podcast](https://saasclub.io/podcast/commandbar-james-evans-385/). The same speaker recalls
"like 200 of those emails and got like a 20 to 30% reply rate" (self-reported, from memory).

> "When anyone agreed to try Stripe they'd say "Right then, give me your laptop" and set them up on the spot."

[Paul Graham, Do Things That Don't Scale](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html).

## 4. Show the best part first
> "winning demos begin with the most valuable part."

[Gong, sales demos](https://www.gong.io/blog/sales-demos). General sales data, not specific to engineers.

## 5. Numbers beat adjectives
> "instead of saying your API is "fast", specify that it delivers 50ms p99 latency."

[daily.dev, technical marketing to developers](https://business.daily.dev/resources/technical-marketing-to-developers-engineers-buyers/). The figure is their illustration.
Only state a number your own build produced.

## 6. Ask for one decision
> "the optimum number of decisions you ask me to make in an email is one."

[Patrick McKenzie, standing invitation](https://www.kalzumeus.com/standing-invitation/).

> "if I see "Book a demo" or "Talk to Sales" I am out"

[Developers quoted by EveryDeveloper](https://everydeveloper.com/blog/devtools-marketing-that-works-according-to-developers-themselves/). Move: offer the thing to try, then the call.

## 7. More than one person per company
> "multi-threading boosts win rates by 130% in deals over $50K."

[Gong, sales insights](https://www.gong.io/blog/the-best-sales-insights-of-2025). A correlation in larger deals.

## Limits
- Lavender's 231,818-email report covers every department; the technical-buyers page cites it and gives 5.2% replies for engineering and product. It is not a technical-only sample.
- No published word-for-word transcript of an engineer-to-engineer cold video was found. The podcast account is a spoken reconstruction.
- Nothing found measures developer-job platforms specifically.
